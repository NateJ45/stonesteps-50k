Who runs it

Your race director

David Corfman has directed this race for over twenty years, which is most of the time it has existed. He is also the 34th runner in history to finish a hundred hundred-mile races, so the person setting the cutoffs has stood where you will be standing at four in the afternoon. He now wears a Coros watch!

Everything the site cannot answer yet, he can. Parking, packet pickup, the briefing: ask, and the answer goes on this site.

What to expect

Roots, rocks, and one very good park

01

Single track, start to finish

Hilly trail with roots, rocks and the occasional tree blow down. Nothing about this course is flat, and very little of it is forgiving.

02

Aid at the end of every loop

Because the course is a loop stack out of The Oval, you pass through aid seven times on the 50K. Drop bags stay in one place all day.

03

1,469.9 acres of forest

Mt. Airy Forest is larger than Central Park's 843 acres. Evergreens, hardwoods, meadows, river crossings, hill climbs and ridge traverses.

04

It gives back

Every year the race donates the net proceeds (>$2000) to Cincinnati Parks, the reason this trail stays runnable.

The 27K

Two 5.3 mile loops alternating with two 3.2 mile loops, on exactly the same trail. You turn for home at 17 miles. Same climbs, four loops instead of seven.

Aid

At the end of every loop, because the course is a stack out of The Oval. On the 50K you pass through it seven times, and drop bags stay in one place all day.

Start and finish

The Oval, Area 13, inside Mt. Airy Forest. Both distances start and finish in the same place. The 50K goes at 8:00 am, the 27K at 8:30.

Cutoff

The course closes at approximately 4:30 pm, eight and a half hours after the 50K start.

The climbing

About 5,200 feet of it

The Oval sits near the top of the course, so every loop drops away from it and climbs back. The big ones save the worst for last: the final half mile climbs at least 170 feet, four times over. Nothing about this course is flat.

Before you write

Answered already

What does my entry include?

A race t-shirt and a timing chip, plus aid at the end of every loop. Entries also fund the annual donation to Cincinnati Parks.

Where does the race start?

The Oval, Area 13, inside Mt. Airy Forest in Cincinnati. Both distances start and finish in the same place, and you pass back through it at the end of every loop.

How much is it, and when do prices go up?

The 50K is $35 through January 31, $50 from February 1 to September 30 and $55 from October 1 to race day. The 27K is $30, $40 and $45 on the same dates. Both plus a processing fee. The 50K is capped at 120 entries and the 27K at 130.

What time does it start?

The 50K starts at 8:00 am and the 27K at 8:30 am. Trekkers may start an hour earlier in each case, and 27K trekkers are not eligible for age group or overall awards. The course closes at about 4:30 pm.

What is the course actually like?

Hilly single track with roots, rocks and the occasional tree blow down. The 50K is four 5.3 mile loops alternating with three 3.2 mile loops, for about 5,200 feet of climbing and 10,344 feet of total elevation change. Nothing about it is flat.

Where do I find past results?

The records page carries course and age-group records for both distances, calculated from the finishing times themselves. The complete archive, including years this site does not hold, lives on RunSignUp.

Why it exists

Over $2,000 a year to Cincinnati Parks

Mt. Airy Forest is 1,469.9 acres, larger than Central Park. The race has run here without a break for over twenty years, and every entry helps keep the trail runnable for everyone who uses it the rest of the year.

What to pack, going by the record

At the 8 am start (coldest 37°F)
The usual start is 46°F. Gloves and a light layer, and you will want the layer off by mid-morning.
By the afternoon
It climbs about 15° by the afternoon, to the low 60s. Wear a layer you can tie round your waist.
Underfoot
The steps were wet in 12 of 23 years. Shoes with real lugs, and dry socks for after.

Kit

What to bring

  • Trail shoes with real lugs

    Roots, rocks and wet leaves in late October. Road shoes will get you round and will not enjoy it.

  • A handheld or a vest

    Aid is only at The Oval, so a long loop is five plus miles between refills.

  • One drop bag

    It stays at The Oval all day and you pass it every loop, so it does the work a crew would.

  • Layers you can shed

    An October morning in Cincinnati starts cold and rarely stays that way.
